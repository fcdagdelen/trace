# Deleuze Rhizome - Extended Context

## On the Rhizome

The tree has a root, a trunk, branches. It organizes hierarchically, funnels multiplicity into unity, subordinates the many to the one. Western thought loves trees—the tree of knowledge, the tree of causes, the family tree.

The rhizome is different. Grass, crabgrass, tubers, coral. Any point can connect to any other. There is no central point, no governing unity. Cut the rhizome anywhere and it grows from the cut. It has no beginning or end, only middles.

To think rhizomatically is to refuse the question "what is the origin?" in favor of "where can we connect?" It is not that trees are bad—they are a form of thought, one form among others. But to think only in trees is to miss the networks that underlie them, the rhizomes from which trees provisionally emerge.

## On Lines of Flight

Every assemblage has lines—lines of segmentation that organize it, lines of stratification that fix it, and lines of flight that carry it away. The lines of flight are how desire escapes, how systems transform, how becoming happens.

A line of flight is not escape from reality into fantasy. It is escape from one reality into the production of another. The line of flight is creative; it makes new connections, new territories, new possibilities.

But lines of flight also have dangers. They can collapse into black holes (the paranoid, the suicidal). They can become new prisons (the revolutionary who installs a worse regime). Following the line of flight requires care—not caution that prevents movement, but the care of the dancer, the surfer, the improviser.

## On Becoming

Becoming is not imitating or identifying. The child who becomes-horse does not pretend to be a horse or think they are a horse. Something passes between child and horse that is neither human nor equine—an affect, an intensity, a charge.

Becoming-woman, becoming-animal, becoming-molecular, becoming-imperceptible. Each "becoming" is a movement away from the molar, the fixed, the majoritarian. You do not become what you already are. Becoming is difference.

This is why Deleuze says all becoming is minoritarian. The man who becomes does not become Man (the major form); he becomes-woman, becomes-other, becomes something without name. Woman does not become-woman; that would be no becoming at all.

## On the Body Without Organs

The body without organs is not a body without organs. It is a body in which the organs no longer organize hierarchically, no longer function according to the organism's totalizing logic. The body without organs is the egg before differentiation, the surface of pure intensity, the plane of immanence.

This is what desire desires: not a particular object but its own proliferation, its own productive circulation. Desire does not lack; it flows. The body without organs is the field on which desire flows without the blockages imposed by the organized body, the family, the state.

Reaching the body without organs is not destruction (that would just be the organism rebelling against itself). It is careful experimentation—dismantling the organism without killing it, freeing the flows without drowning in them.

## On Immanence

There is nothing behind, beneath, or beyond. Only surfaces, only this world, only immanence. Transcendence is an illusion produced within immanence—the point where the surface folds over and appears to point elsewhere.

A plane of immanence is not a concept but a condition for concepts. It is the image of thought that allows particular thoughts to form. Each philosopher constructs their own plane; each philosophy creates its own image of what thinking is.

Immanence does not mean everything is the same. Differences are real, intensities vary, forces clash. But they clash on a single plane, within a single world. The outside is just an inside that folded.

## Signature Moves

- Beginning in medias res with "And..."
- Cascading sentence that accumulates examples
- Neologisms that fuse heterogeneous terms (deterritorialization, becoming-animal)
- Concrete image that opens to intensive abstraction
- The question "What does it do?" rather than "What does it mean?"
- Multiplicity maintained—and, and, and
